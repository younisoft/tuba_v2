## 2. Gaps present in Tuba that are NOT shared by either competitor (Tuba-specific deficits)

These are found in Tuba's own codebase with no equivalent finding in either the Bayut or Aqar audits, meaning Tuba is *behind* the field here, not merely at parity with a weak field:

1. **Critical security posture** — universal hardcoded password, unconditional OTP-bypass, unverified Nafath signature, TLS disabled on payments, unauthenticated payment fulfilment, a mass-delete IDOR. Neither competitor audit reported findings of this severity class (both audits were UX/product/architecture focused rather than penetration tests, so this isn't a like-for-like comparison, but it is a Tuba-specific, source-confirmed risk that should weigh heavily in any "who's ahead" narrative).
2. **A live notification-misrouting bug** actively sending the wrong party the lead notification — a functional regression beyond "shallow CRM," it's a broken CRM in the specific path affected.
3. **Checkout/dependency inconsistency** (see [12_SOURCE_CODE_ASSESSMENT.md](12_SOURCE_CODE_ASSESSMENT.md) §0) — a currently-observed risk of fatal errors on media upload, receipt generation, and Excel import in this exact checkout state, not found or applicable to either competitor's audit scope.
4. **~30% dead/commented-out code** in the largest controllers, two full duplicate media-helper files, dual property-form UIs — general code-health debt neither competitor audit characterizes at this depth (different audit methodology), but real and specific to Tuba's engineering hygiene as documented across two independent audits one month apart.

## 3. Gaps present in ALL THREE platforms (genuine market-wide opportunity)

Per the TBX synthesis (`competitor-analysis/TUBA_BROKER_EXPERIENCE_FOUNDATION.md` §1, §6), and confirmed applicable to Tuba by this pass:

1. **No platform has real RBAC.** This is the single largest opportunity in the entire three-way comparison — building genuine role templates, scoped visibility, and delegated authority as a first-class primitive would put Tuba ahead of both incumbents simultaneously, not just at parity.
2. **No platform explains its numbers.** Whatever KPIs/status pills a next-gen Tuba dashboard ships (license state, quality score, conversion rate, wallet balance) should ship with plain-language "what this means, what to do" — a shared failure across Bayut, Aqar, and (per the July audit's "decorative dashboard tiles" finding) Tuba's current admin/agent dashboards too.
3. **No platform has a real CRM.** Two-way messaging, lead scoring, SLA tracking, and pipeline stages are absent from all three — Tuba's `PropertyRequestOffer` unpopulated-fields finding shows this was designed for at some point and never finished, which is arguably a *head start* over building from zero, if resumed.
4. **Compliance UX is universally under-explained.** Whatever government-paperwork requirements a platform gates on (REGA/Nafath/FAL for Tuba, Wasata/deed-number for Aqar, license fields for Bayut), none front-load or explain them before the user hits the requirement mid-flow.

## 5. Live-session postscript: the checkout-consistency question

[12_SOURCE_CODE_ASSESSMENT.md](12_SOURCE_CODE_ASSESSMENT.md) §0 flagged, as the single largest caveat on this whole assessment, that the local `tuba/` checkout showed signs of an inconsistent framework/dependency state and could not be confirmed to match what's actually deployed to `tuba.com.sa`. The live session partially resolves this in the more concerning direction: navigating to `/properties/create` on the real, live production site returns a genuine **HTTP 500 Server Error**. This does not prove the exact root cause (the missing `intervention/image` dependency identified in source), since the live product's actual entry point is a different, modal-based flow that was not fully exercised (see [06_WORKFLOW_ANALYSIS.md](06_WORKFLOW_ANALYSIS.md) §1) — but it does confirm that **production itself, not just this local checkout, has at least one broken core route**, which raises rather than lowers the urgency of Phase 0 in [17_IMPLEMENTATION_PRIORITIES.md](17_IMPLEMENTATION_PRIORITIES.md).
