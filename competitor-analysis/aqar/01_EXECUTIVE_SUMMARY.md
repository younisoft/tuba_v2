# Executive Summary

Aqar (تطبيق عقار) is a Saudi real estate marketplace whose broker-facing tooling is split across **two parallel surfaces that duplicate each other**: a consumer-style account drawer (My Ads, Subscription Info, Wallet, Featured Campaigns) and a separate "Office Management" console (`/offices-management`) with its own sidebar exposing Listings, Wallet, Featured Campaigns, Statistics, Users, Invoices, and Settings — the same capabilities, reachable two different ways, under two different navigation models. Where Bayut Profolio (see `../../product-audit/`) presents as a single dedicated agency back-office, Aqar reads as a classifieds marketplace that grew broker tooling out from underneath its consumer shopping experience, and the seams still show.

Key observations:

- **REGA compliance is load-bearing, not cosmetic.** Every listing-creation path is gated behind a real government-issued ad-license number, sourced either from an existing REGA license or from a "free license" sub-flow that itself demands a live Wasata (brokerage) contract number, a deed number, and a declared price before anything can be posted. Licenses carry **seven** distinct lifecycle states (`Awaiting Information → Awaiting Contract Approval → Contract Issuance in Progress → Licensing in Progress → Awaiting Payment → Licensed → Expired`), a materially more granular pipeline than Bayut's four non-active listing states.
- **Two separate currencies monetize two separate things.** "Ad spaces" (مساحات) are subscription-bundled listing-slot credits (30 on the audited Basic Individual plan, tied to a 1-year term and a 1-seat limit). "Wallet" (المحفظة) is a **separate, cash-loaded balance** for booking fees and promotional spend — and unlike a simple credit balance, wallet top-ups **expire** (~3 months observed: a 100 SAR top-up on 21/04/26 lapsed by 22/07/26 with no visible warning before expiry). This creates a genuine urgency mechanic Bayut's single fungible credit pool doesn't have.
- **District Broker (وسيط الحي) is Aqar's pay-to-win answer to Bayut's merit-based TruBroker.** Brokers bid to be the featured broker for a city zone (Riyadh: North/East/West/South/Central — five broad zones, not fine-grained neighborhoods, despite the feature's name) for a one-week window. Where TruBroker converts listing quality and responsiveness into a public trust badge, District Broker converts marketing spend directly into visibility — no quality or performance gate observed.
- **A real two-sided lead marketplace exists and is under-surfaced.** The account's pre-existing Favorites included live marketplace listings tagged "طلب تسويق" (Marketing Request) — property owners posting a request for a broker to market their property, discoverable and (implicitly) claimable by any broker browsing the marketplace. This is a genuine demand-generation mechanic with no direct Bayut equivalent observed, but it lives inside a generic "Favorites" list with no dedicated broker-facing inbox, filter, or claim workflow.
- **Ejar (government rental-contract platform) integration is deeper than Bayut's**, with a paid documentation service (SAR 299 residential / SAR 499 commercial) to notarize a contract through Aqar, plus a dedicated "Broker Ejar Contracts" view that tracks assigned contracts with a conversion-rate KPI — a real funnel metric no equivalent of which appears in the Bayut audit.
- **Team management is flatter than Bayut's.** Adding a team member is a single "phone number" input with no visible role, permission, or license-sharing control at all — even Bayut's binary license-sharing toggle is absent here.
- **Empty states range from good to broken.** Saved Searches has a clean, well-worded empty state with a clear CTA. The Featured Campaigns flow, by contrast, dead-ends: "Choose a listing from your list" renders with no listings, no empty-state message, and no path to create one first — a genuine workflow break, not a design choice.
- **The private broker back-office is instrumented like a public marketing page.** Even on `/offices-management` (an authenticated, non-marketing screen), the network log shows Google Ads conversion pixels, Google Analytics, a Snapchat pixel, LinkedIn Insight Tag, and Microsoft Clarity session recording all firing — attribution/analytics tooling built for acquisition funnels, running on logged-in operational screens.

Overall read: Aqar has **real, distinctive commercial mechanics** (wallet expiry, license-state granularity, bid-based visibility, paid Ejar notarization, marketing-request lead flow) that Bayut does not have — but it lacks Bayut's one coherent, purpose-built back-office feel. Tuba's opportunity is not to copy either: it's to take Aqar's sharper compliance/monetization primitives and Bayut's more coherent single-console information architecture, then fix what both leave broken (dead-end empty states, flat/no-role team management, fragmented reporting) with real guidance and automation.
