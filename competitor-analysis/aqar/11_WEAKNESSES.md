# Weaknesses

- **Two parallel, duplicate systems for the same core operations.** Listings, Wallet, and Featured Campaigns are each reachable from both the consumer-style account drawer and the separate Office Management console, at different URLs, with no observed cross-linking — a broker has no single canonical place to manage their business.
- **A genuine dead-end workflow**: `/user/campaigns/new` renders only a heading with a completely blank body when the account has no listings — no picker, no empty-state message, no "create a listing first" CTA. This is not a minor rough edge; it is a broken step in a paid-feature funnel.
- **Team management has no access control of any kind.** Adding a team member is a bare phone-number field — no role, no permission, no license-sharing toggle. This is flatter than even Bayut's binary license-sharing toggle, which the Bayut audit itself already flags as too coarse.
- **Wallet balances expire with no observed pre-expiry warning.** A real 100 SAR top-up lapsed unused roughly three months after purchase, and no notification setting, banner, or reminder mechanism for impending wallet expiry was found anywhere in the product — this is silent forfeiture of the broker's own money, not a communicated feature.
- **The District Broker feature's name overstates its granularity.** "وسيط الحي" (Neighborhood Broker) implies district/neighborhood-level targeting, but the actual selectable unit is one of ~5 broad compass-direction zones per city — a real mismatch between feature branding and implementation that will frustrate a broker expecting to target a specific area.
- **Billing/invoice history shows no transaction amount and no downloadable invoice.** The one real billing entry observed (VISA, "Annual subscription to Basic package," timestamped) carried no visible price — a materially thinner financial record than expected of paid SaaS billing history.
- **A live, unresolved navigation bug**: the account drawer's "English" locale link routes to `/user/bookings` instead of switching locale — the only accessible-language control observed in the drawer does not do what it says.
- **No bulk actions anywhere.** Every table observed (Team, Statistics, Wallet history, Broker Ejar Contracts) supports single-row actions only — no multi-select, no bulk edit/delete/export, a real efficiency gap for any office managing more than a handful of records at once.
- **Compliance forms carry zero inline guidance.** The free-license form demands a Wasata contract number and a deed number with no tooltip, help link, or explanation of where to find either — a broker unfamiliar with this specific terminology has no in-product path to self-serve.
- **A private, authenticated back-office page ships a full consumer ad-attribution stack.** Google Ads conversion pixels, a Snapchat pixel, LinkedIn Insight Tag, and repeated Microsoft Clarity session-recording beacons were all observed firing on `/offices-management` — infrastructure built for acquisition funnels, running on a screen a broker only reaches after logging in to manage their own subscription and team.
- **Aqar+ is a consumer product surfaced inside the broker's own account drawer**, with every listed perk benefiting a property seeker rather than the broker managing inventory — a structural blurring of the platform's two sides inside one navigation system.
- **Inconsistent "currently selected" visual language.** The Office Management sidebar marks its active item with a grey fill, while every tab-pill filter elsewhere in the product (My Ads, Licenses, Wallet, Bookings, District Broker) uses a solid green fill for the same concept — a small but real internal design-system inconsistency.
- **No visible role/permission model surfaces on any first-level screen**, mirroring the Bayut audit's equivalent finding for Bayut, but with even less exposed here — no per-staff credit limit, no license-sharing toggle, nothing beyond the ability to add or presumably remove a phone number.
- **Error-state UX is entirely unassessed** in this audit because no error condition was naturally triggered — this is a genuine coverage gap that should be closed with a targeted follow-up pass (e.g., deliberately submitting invalid data) before treating "no errors observed" as "errors are well-handled."
